# ADR 0040: Recurring Follow-up Scheduler

## Status

Accepted. Issue: [#463](https://github.com/stevdrey/dokene/issues/463) (Phase 4 A4), part of epic [#457](https://github.com/stevdrey/dokene/issues/457).

It composes [ADR 0036](0036-deterministic-recurring-follow-up-candidates.md) (candidates), [ADR 0037](0037-durable-multi-instance-follow-up-work-claiming.md) (work ledger) and [ADR 0039](0039-bounded-follow-up-retries-and-explicit-recovery.md) (retries and recovery), which stay unchanged. It reads provider health through the ADR 0039 port ([ADR 0038](0038-provider-health-gate.md)) and the rate and abuse budget (B2 [#459](https://github.com/stevdrey/dokene/issues/459)) through a new port.

## Context

A1 to A3 can record due cycles, claim them across instances and classify each attempt, but nothing runs them. Phase 4 needs a recurring job that does, across every active tenant, without automating judgment or sending: an operator still decides and sends every follow-up (ADR 0013, ADR 0028). The job must be off until an operator turns it on, bounded on every axis, fair between tenants, safe under several instances and restarts, and stoppable.

## Decision

1. **One coordinator, off by default.** `FollowUpSchedulingCoordinator` runs one tick every `dokene.follow-up.scheduler.interval` (default 1 minute, 10 seconds to 1 hour, fixed delay). Ticks run on a thread of their own, which is started only while the scheduler is enabled. A long tick therefore never delays the application's shared `@Scheduled` jobs, such as dispatch recovery and the webhook inbox. An exception in a tick is logged, and later ticks still run. `dokene.follow-up.scheduler.enabled` (default `false`) is the master switch. Beneath it, `generation-enabled` and `processing-enabled` (default `true`) are kill switches for each stage. All three are read once at startup, so changing one needs a restart. No API, tenant setting or header can change them. While the master switch is off, or both stage switches are, a tick reads and writes nothing; it does not even list tenants.
2. **What a tick does per tenant.** In order: one candidate scan (ADR 0036, when generation is on); then, when processing is on, one enqueue (ADR 0037), one budget admission and one `FollowUpWorkProcessor.process` batch (ADR 0039). Each stage fails on its own. Its exception is counted and logged by class only, and the next stage and the next tenant still run. A tenant that the scan finds inactive is neither enqueued nor processed.
3. **Bounded.** A tick visits at most `tenants-per-tick` tenants (default 50, 1 to 500). It claims at most `work-batch-size` items per tenant (default 10, 1 to 200, and never above `dokene.follow-up.work.batch-size`; startup fails otherwise while processing is enabled). It starts no new tenant once `max-tick-duration` (default 30 seconds, 1 second to 30 minutes) has passed. The tenant in progress always finishes, and its own work is bounded by the two batch sizes. Provider health still narrows a batch to one probe claim, or to none (ADR 0039 decision 4).
4. **Fair tenant rotation.** Tenants are visited in id order after an in-memory cursor. The cursor advances before each visit, so the next tick resumes after the last tenant started, even when that tenant failed or the tick was cut short. A page shorter than `tenants-per-tick` ends the rotation, and the next tick starts again from the first tenant. Every active tenant is therefore visited at least once in any `floor(active tenants / tenants-per-tick) + 1` consecutive uncut ticks, however large or broken any one tenant is. When the count is an exact multiple of `tenants-per-tick`, the last of those ticks reads an empty page and only restarts the rotation. The cursor is not durable: a restarted instance starts from the first tenant, which only reorders work.
5. **Trusted tenant scope only.** A tick must start outside any tenant scope. Tenant ids come only from `dokene.find_follow_up_scheduling_tenants` (V51), a `SECURITY DEFINER` finder like the V35 maintenance finders. It needs a short-lived maintenance capability signed for its own task (`FOLLOW_UP_SCHEDULING`), returns active tenant ids only, one keyset page of at most 500, and is executable only by the runtime role. Every later call binds the tenant itself under forced RLS (ADR 0036, ADR 0037). No tenant id comes from a request or a payload.
6. **Several instances, no leader.** Each instance runs its own coordinator, with its own `worker-id` label on its claims (a 1–64 character label such as a pod name, never tenant or personal data; a random `dokene-…` label when unset). Correctness comes from the database: candidates deduplicate on their cycle, one item per customer stays open, and claims are fenced and `SKIP LOCKED`. Instances may visit the same tenant at once. Their scans then record each cycle once and their claims never take the same item. No advisory lock or leader election is needed.
7. **No overlapping ticks.** Within one instance, a tick that starts while another is running does nothing and returns `OVERLAPPING`.
8. **Graceful shutdown and restart.** The coordinator is a `SmartLifecycle` bean. Stopping it schedules no further tick, starts no new tenant (even if the bean is started again before that tick ends), never interrupts the tick in progress, and waits up to `shutdown-timeout` (default 30 seconds, 0 to 5 minutes) for the tenant in progress. Ticks after that return `STOPPED`. A claim left behind by a stopped, killed or crashed instance is not taken over until its lease expires; another instance, or the restarted one, then takes it over as the next attempt (ADR 0037 decision 5, ADR 0039 decision 7).
9. **Budget admission port.** Before claiming, the coordinator asks `FollowUpWorkAdmission.admit(tenant, requested)` how many items the tenant may claim this tick. B2 ([#459](https://github.com/stevdrey/dokene/issues/459)) owns the policy and adapter. Until an adapter exists, the coordinator admits the configured batch, which stays bounded by decision 3. An exception, or an answer outside `0..requested`, admits nothing for that tenant this tick (fail closed). A denial claims nothing, so it spends no attempt; work already enqueued stays `PENDING`.
10. **The Phase 4 handler records only.** `RecordOnlyFollowUpWorkHandler` completes each claimed item without calling `beginEffect`, so no external effect can begin. Completing the item closes the cycle in the ledger and collapses the customer's older candidates into it (ADR 0037 decision 2). It does not change the operator due queue (ADR 0013), which stays computed from the customer's policy. A completed cycle is settled for good: no later handler acts on it. Phase 5 automation will replace this handler with one that calls `beginEffect` and re-checks every last-moment gate (ADR 0028) first, under its own decision.
11. **Injected clock.** Tick budgets, scans, claims and leases use the application `Clock`, so tests control them, and instances must keep their clocks close, as ADR 0037 decision 7 requires.
12. **A record, not an action.** The scheduler approves, creates and sends nothing, calls no provider and writes no audit events. Logs carry counts, the worker label, tenant ids and exception classes, never exception messages or personal data.

## Enabling

Keep the scheduler disabled until all of the following hold:

- an adapter maps ADR 0038 provider health onto `FollowUpProviderHealth` (otherwise every batch is a single probe claim, as ADR 0039 notes);
- the B2 budget adapter implements `FollowUpWorkAdmission`;
- the integrated Phase 4 certification ([#466](https://github.com/stevdrey/dokene/issues/466)) has passed, and after a restore, reconciliation is done and an operator has approved enabling it.

Enabling processing in Phase 4 cannot be undone for the ledger. The record-only handler completes every due cycle it claims, and completion collapses the customer's older candidates into it (decision 10, ADR 0037 decision 2). A later effectful handler, such as Phase 5 automation, therefore never acts on those cycles. Keep `processing-enabled` off until that outcome is intended, for example by enabling generation alone.

## Consequences

- Recurring candidate generation and ledger processing are available but inert by default. Enabling them affects only the ledger: no message is created, approved or sent, and operators keep working from the due queue.
- A tick is bounded in tenants, items and time, so one slow or failing tenant delays the others by at most one tenant's bounded work.
- The cursor lives in memory, so instances may visit tenants in the same order. This is safe (decision 6), and the database shares out their claims.
- Bounded counts by state, last run outcome and metrics belong to B3 ([#462](https://github.com/stevdrey/dokene/issues/462)). The tick summary is a log line, not a metric.
- V51 is additive. Rolling back to a release without the coordinator leaves the finder unused.

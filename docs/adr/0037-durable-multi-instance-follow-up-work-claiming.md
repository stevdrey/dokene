# ADR 0037: Durable Multi-instance Follow-up Work Claiming

## Status

Accepted. Issue: [#458](https://github.com/stevdrey/dokene/issues/458) (Phase 4 A2), part of epic [#457](https://github.com/stevdrey/dokene/issues/457).

It builds on [ADR 0036](0036-deterministic-recurring-follow-up-candidates.md) (candidates), which stays unchanged except for an additive unique key.

## Context

Candidates (A1) record that a customer's cycle is due. Several backend instances share one PostgreSQL database, so the step that acts on a candidate must be claimed by exactly one instance at a time, survive a crash of that instance, and never let an instance that lost its claim overwrite the work of the instance that took over. Retry policy and recovery (A3 [#461](https://github.com/stevdrey/dokene/issues/461)) and scheduling (A4 [#463](https://github.com/stevdrey/dokene/issues/463)) build on this ledger.

## Decision

1. **One ledger row per candidate.** `dokene.follow_up_work_items` (V48) is keyed by the candidate id and references the candidate together with the customer and due date it copies (a new unique key on `follow_up_candidates` makes that reference possible), so the copies cannot drift. States: `PENDING`, `CLAIMED`, `COMPLETED`, `RETRY_DUE`, `EXHAUSTED` (recovery required). Check constraints tie each state to its columns: a claim has a token, a worker, a claim instant and a later lease expiry; `COMPLETED` and `EXHAUSTED` have a finish instant; `RETRY_DUE` and `EXHAUSTED` have a safe failure category.
2. **A customer has at most one open item.** A partial unique index on `(tenant_id, customer_id)` enforces it for `PENDING`, `CLAIMED` and `RETRY_DUE` items and for `EXHAUSTED` items whose outcome is unknown (`OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` or `LEASE_EXPIRED`). Enqueueing (`FollowUpWorkLedger.enqueue`) takes, for each customer without open work, the latest due cycle that has no item yet, in `due_date, customer_id` order, at most one batch per call, and only for an active tenant. A finished (completed or exhausted), non-collapsed item covers the customer's candidates that the database recorded (`follow_up_candidates.recorded_at`, added by V48) by the time it recorded the item's latest claim (`claim_recorded_at`). Covered candidates are never enqueued: completion records up to 100 of them, and each enqueue first records up to one batch of any that remain, as `COMPLETED` with `collapsed_into` naming the covering candidate. So cycles split by a cadence or time zone change (ADR 0036) are never separate follow-ups, and an exhausted follow-up is not retried through another of its candidates. Coverage compares database instants only, so clock skew between instances cannot hide a new cycle. An item exhausted with an unknown outcome keeps the slot, so newer work for that customer waits until recovery resolves it rather than risking a second send after an ambiguous one; an item exhausted by safe failures (`PROCESSING_ERROR`, `DEPENDENCY_UNAVAILABLE`) releases it. An index over finished, non-collapsed items serves the coverage lookup. Because enqueue reads coverage from its statement snapshot, an enqueue that started before a newer cover finished can still insert a covered candidate as `PENDING` after that cover released the customer; every claim therefore first resolves covered, never-claimed `PENDING` items into their cover (bounded) and never claims one, since any statement that sees such an item started after its cover finished. A collapsed item's `collapsed_into` must name a candidate of the same customer (composite foreign key).
3. **Atomic, bounded claims.** `claim(tenant, worker, limit)` runs one `UPDATE … FROM (SELECT … ORDER BY due_date, customer_id LIMIT n FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED)`. Claimable items are pending or retry due at `now`, or claimed with a lease that expired at or before `now`, with attempts left. Concurrent instances never wait on or take the same row, and PostgreSQL re-checks the claimable condition on a row that changed before it was locked. The limit is between 1 and `dokene.follow-up.work.batch-size` (default 20, at most 200). Suspended or archived tenants get no claims.
4. **Lease and fencing identity.** A claim increments `attempts` and stores a fresh `gen_random_uuid()` token, the worker id (a 1–64 character label such as a pod name, never tenant or personal data) and `lease_expires_at = now + dokene.follow-up.work.lease-duration` (default 2 minutes, 10 seconds to 1 hour). The attempt number plus the token is the claim's fencing identity. Renewal, completion, failure and recovery each match the candidate, state `CLAIMED`, attempt, token and `lease_expires_at > now` in a single `UPDATE`, so a claimant whose lease expired, or whose item another instance claimed again, writes nothing and receives `STALE`. Renewal extends from `now` and never shortens a lease. An expired but untaken claim is stale too: the holder cannot know whether another instance is about to take over. The fence also matches the customer and due date the claim was issued with, so a claim object carrying another customer's metadata is stale, and a completion's collapse takes the customer and claim instant from the completed row, never from the caller. Each outcome first locks the claim's row (`SELECT … FOR UPDATE`, which waits for any transaction holding it), then reads the clock and judges the lease, then runs the fenced update with that instant in the same transaction: an outcome that started before expiry but waited past it on the row lock is stale.
5. **Retries and exhaustion.** `fail(claim, failure, retryAt)` makes the item `RETRY_DUE` from `retryAt` while attempts remain and `EXHAUSTED` once `dokene.follow-up.work.max-attempts` (default 3, at most 20) claims were made. It accepts only failures with no possible external effect; `OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` must go through `requireRecovery`, which exhausts the item at once and never schedules a replay. Before claiming, items that expired on their last attempt, and due retries that exceed a lowered limit, are exhausted (`LEASE_EXPIRED` for an expired claim). Backoff choice and recovery belong to A3.
6. **Trusted tenant scope only.** Every ledger call must start outside any tenant scope, binds the given tenant (or the claim's tenant) with `TenantContextProvider.callWithTenantId`, and runs in one short transaction under forced RLS. Statements also name the tenant explicitly. The runtime role may `SELECT`, `INSERT` and `UPDATE` its own tenant's items and can never delete one. A claim relabelled with another tenant finds no row under that tenant's scope.
7. **Lease instants come from the injected clock.** Claims, leases and outcomes use the calling instance's `Clock`, so tests control expiry and A4 injects it. Instances must keep their clocks within a small fraction of the lease duration of each other; the schema has no cross-instant ordering check because instants may come from different instances. Coverage (decision 2) does not depend on these clocks: it compares instants the database records itself.
8. **A record, not an action.** The ledger approves, creates or sends nothing, calls no provider, writes no audit events and adds no scheduler. A claim holder must still re-evaluate the candidate and every last-moment consent, do-not-contact, approval, authorization and kill-switch check (ADR 0028) before any external effect.

## Consequences

- Correctness needs no leader election or advisory lock: row locks, the unique open-item index and the fenced updates hold under any number of instances.
- A crashed instance delays its items by at most one lease duration; each takeover consumes one attempt.
- A holder that works longer than its lease must renew; if it misses the window it must stop and may not commit, even when nobody has taken the item yet.
- `items(tenant, limit)` is an unpaged diagnostic read. Bounded counts by state, oldest due age and metrics belong to B3 ([#462](https://github.com/stevdrey/dokene/issues/462)).
- Items, like candidates, are never deleted by the runtime; retention is an operator or migration-role decision.
